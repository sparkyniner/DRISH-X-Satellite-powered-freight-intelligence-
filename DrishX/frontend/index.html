Getting Started

Introduction

DrishX — see what's moving, anywhere, for free.

DrishX answers one question for any road on Earth: how much traffic is on it, and how has that changed over time?

It exploits a quirk in how the Sentinel-2 satellite captures imagery. The sensor records red, green, and blue light about 1.01 seconds apart. Anything stationary looks normal — but a vehicle moving at highway speed shifts position between those captures, leaving a distinctive blue→green→red spectral smear across a few pixels. DrishX finds those smears, counts them, estimates speed and heading, and tracks how volume changes across weeks and months.

The output is a traffic-activity time-series for any major corridor on the planet — built entirely on free Copernicus data, running locally in your browser, with zero ground infrastructure.

Why it matters — Road traffic is one of the most reliable observable indicators of what's actually happening in a place: more useful than official statements, harder to fake than statistics. DrishX lets you point at any road, anywhere, and get months of trend data in minutes.

Quick Start

Prerequisites

  • Python 3.11 (other versions may not work)
  • A free Copernicus Data Space account (~2 minutes)
  • The trained RF model file (in the DrishX/ subdirectory)
Heads up: first runs can be slow while DrishX pings Overpass mirrors for road data. Be patient.

Install and run

# Clone the repository
git clone https://github.com/sparkyniner/DRISH-X-Satellite-powered-freight-intelligence-.git
cd DRISH-X-Satellite-powered-freight-intelligence-

# Create and activate a virtual environment
python3.11 -m venv venv
source venv/bin/activate        # macOS / Linux
venv\Scripts\activate           # Windows

cd DrishX
pip install -r requirements.txt
python drishx.py

The server starts on port 8000 — open http://localhost:8000.

Next: connect Copernicus Link your free satellite credentials to go live.

Connecting Copernicus

DrishX runs on free Copernicus (Sentinel-2) satellite imagery. To pull it, you'll generate an OAuth Client ID and Client Secret once, then paste them into the app. The whole thing takes about two minutes.

1

Sign in to Copernicus Data Space

Create an account (or log in) at the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem. It's completely free.

identity.dataspace.copernicus.eu
2

Open User Settings

Once you're signed in you'll land on the Dashboard. In the left-hand side panel, scroll to the bottom and open User Settings.

3

Create an OAuth client

Find the OAuth clients card on the right side of the screen and click Create new OAuth client.

4

Name it and copy your keys

Give the client a name (e.g. DrishX) and click Create. Copernicus will display your Client ID and Client Secret.

Copy the Client Secret immediately. It's shown only once — if you navigate away without saving it, you'll have to create a new client to get a new one.
5

Connect DrishX

Back in DrishX, the connect window opens automatically on first launch — or click Connect on the Copernicus Link card in the sidebar. Paste your Client ID and Client Secret and click Connect. Once it confirms, live telemetry is online — no config files needed.

Prefer environment variables? Instead of the UI, copy .env.example to .env and fill in your keys there. Both methods work.

The Interface

DrishX runs directly in the browser with minimal setup.

Selecting an Area of Interest (AOI)

Click Draw AOI, then drag to define your analysis region. DrishX automatically detects road corridors in the area, fetches satellite data, and begins analysis.

Recommended settings

  • Mode: Dense Mode for maximum data coverage (more images processed → more reliable detection, especially under cloud).
  • Timeline: Short (1–2 weeks) for quick signals · Medium (1–3 months) for trend detection · Long (3–6 months) for a strong baseline + anomaly detection.

Data storage

Cached data and detection outputs default to drishx_data/ in the project directory. Redirect it (e.g. to a larger external drive) with:

export DRISHX_DATA_DIR=/path/to/your/storage

Use Cases

  • Economic intelligence — Truck traffic is an honest economic signal. Port throughput drops show up in departing vehicles before any press release; DrishX gives a proxy for economic activity that refreshes every ~5 days, anywhere on Earth.
  • Supply chain & logistics — Monitor corridor congestion without your own fleet data, validate traffic projections against 6 months of satellite history, benchmark seasonal patterns across competing routes.
  • Trade & sanctions monitoring — Rerouting from tariffs or sanctions shows on corridors before trade databases. Watch key crossings and compare parallel routes to spot diversion.
  • Security & defense intelligence — Track changes in vehicle volume on access, supply, and perimeter roads over time. DrishX reports volume, speed, heading, and trend — not vehicle identity. At 10 m resolution a military truck looks identical to a civilian one; it is not a surveillance system and shouldn't be presented as one.
  • Disaster & crisis response — Compare current activity against a historical baseline to find corridors that have gone quiet (blocked/damaged) or unusually busy (diversion/evacuation).
  • Journalism & investigations — Satellite-derived, timestamped, independently verifiable data from an ESA satellite — not from any party with a stake in the answer.

How It Works

Based on Fisser et al. (2022), adapted for real-time web streaming.

The physics

Sentinel-2 captures spectral bands ~1.01 s apart (blue B02 → red B04). A vehicle at 80 km/h moves ~22 m in that interval; at 10 m resolution it lands in different positions per band, creating the blue→green→red smear DrishX is trained to find.

The pipeline

1

Feature stack

Seven features per pixel: variance of RGB, normalized R/B, normalized G/B, and mean-centered B04 / B03 / B02 / B08.

2

Random Forest classification

Each pixel is classified as background, blue, green, or red — with background confidence thresholded at 0.75.

3

Recursive object extraction

Seed at blue pixels, grow through green then red; validate all three colors are present within a 3–5 px extent, and keep detections scoring above 1.2.

4

Per-detection output

Latitude/longitude, heading, speed estimate, and a confidence score for every detected vehicle.

What it does well

  • Count large vehicles (trucks/buses) on major highways
  • Track volume trends over weeks and months
  • Estimate speed (±15 km/h) and heading (±22.5°)
  • Process multi-month archives in minutes

What it can't do

  • Detect cars (sub-pixel at 10 m)
  • Distinguish vehicle types
  • See through clouds
  • Provide real-time monitoring (5-day revisit + delay)

Technical Notes

Resolution

10 m pixels; a ~18 m truck spans ~2 px, and the motion smear extends it to 3–5 px. Cars (~4.5 m) are sub-pixel and invisible. Smaller vehicles need PlanetScope (3.7 m, same physics, Planet Labs key).

Cloud cover

DrishX uses the Sentinel-2 cloud mask to exclude cloudy pixels; overcast frames correctly show zero detections. Read the moving-average trend, not individual days.

Regional accuracy

Trained on German autobahns. Accuracy varies by region:

European motorways: roughly 70–80% detection with 5–10% false positives. The strongest region, since the model was trained on German autobahns.

Middle East & North Africa: strong results. Arid, high-contrast roads that are rarely cloudy make for excellent imaging conditions.

South & Southeast Asia: mixed. Monsoon season limits the number of usable, cloud-free frames.

Sub-Saharan Africa: good on paved trunk roads, weaker on unpaved surfaces where contrast is lower.
